# Rate limit new meal plans, raise the shared-IP auth limits, and turn on HSTS
Date: 2026-11-20

## Status
Proposed. Responds to findings F-1, F-2 and part of F-4 from the security assessment of 2026-11-19 (commit `0b45fcc`).
Updates [ADR-006](adr-006.md) and [ADR-008](adr-008.md); a dated note in each points here.
Revised 2026-11-20 after the PR #61 review: F-1 also limits planner runs in progress, a refused request no longer
runs the planner to draw its page, and the Meal Plan page's own planner runs (no saved plan, or changed settings)
take the same limits. A second review pass added a separate cap on registration answers that reveal an account
(F-2), and a refused new plan keeps the day the user was looking at. A third counts only the answers that would
reveal an account, so other sign-ups are never refused by that cap.

## Decision

| Finding | Change | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F-1** (Medium): `POST /meal-plan/new/` runs the planner synchronously with no limit, so one account can hold every sync Gunicorn worker | Every planner run, from `POST /meal-plan/new/` or from `GET /meal-plan/` when there is no saved plan for the user's current meal settings, goes through `nutrition.views._run_planner` and two limits, both checked before the planner runs. **In flight:** at most 1 run per user and 2 in total at a time, across all pods (`MEAL_PLAN_CONCURRENCY`); otherwise "A new meal plan is already being made. Please wait a moment and try again." with `Retry-After: 15`, and the request isn't counted. **Per hour:** `AUTH_THROTTLE['meal_plan'] = (10, 60 * 60)` plans made per user, by either route; otherwise "You've asked for a lot of new meal plans. Please wait a while and try again." Both refusals are HTTP 429 and show the saved plan, read with `saved_meal_plan`, which never runs the planner: if the saved plan is missing or no longer matches the user's meal settings, the page says so and offers a reload instead. Showing a saved plan is never limited | `accounts.throttle.meal_plan_slot`, `accounts.throttle.acquire_meal_plan`, `nutrition.services.saved_meal_plan`, `nutrition.views.meal_plan`, `nutrition.views.meal_plan_new` |
| **F-2** (Low): the IP-wide counters lock out everyone behind a shared IP (campus Wi-Fi, dorms, carrier NAT), including logins with the right password, and refuse the 11th sign-up from one IP in an hour | `login_ip` raised from 20 to **100** failed logins per IP per 15 minutes. `register` raised from 10 to **50** registration POSTs per IP per hour. New `register_taken` limit: **10** answers per IP per hour that say a username or email is taken, the old registration budget, so raising `register` doesn't give probing for accounts more room; only answers that would reveal an account are counted, just before they are sent, so other sign-ups are never refused by it. Once it is used up, the taken-username and taken-email errors are left out: with other errors the page shows just those, exactly as for an unused username and email, and with none the request gets the generic 429 (`accounts.views.reveals_an_account`, `hide_account_errors`, `throttle.acquire_register_probe`). The per-identifier limit (5 failed logins per identifier and IP per 15 minutes) is unchanged | `fitpro/settings.py` `AUTH_THROTTLE`, `accounts.throttle.acquire_register`, `accounts.views.register` |
| **F-4** (Low), HSTS part: production sends no `Strict-Transport-Security` header | `SECURE_HSTS_SECONDS = 3600` (one hour), with `includeSubDomains` and `preload` off. `security.W004` is no longer silenced; `security.W005` and `security.W021` (subdomains, preload) are silenced with the reason in `settings.py` | `fitpro/settings.py` |

HSTS is set unconditionally rather than inside the `if BEHIND_HTTPS_PROXY:` block that the assessment suggested.
Django's `SecurityMiddleware` only adds the header to requests it sees as HTTPS. Plain-HTTP local runs, the CI container
smoke test and load balancer health checks (answered by `HealthCheckMiddleware` before `SecurityMiddleware`) never get
it, so the behavior is the same. Setting it unconditionally keeps it next to the other pinned headers and lets an
ordinary test prove it.

**Next step:** once production has served the one-hour header without problems, raise `SECURE_HSTS_SECONDS` to
31536000 (one year) in a follow-up PR.

## What this offers
* **F-1:** planner runs never hold more than 2 of the 4 production workers (2 pods x 2 sync workers), however many
  requests arrive at once or from how many accounts, and one account runs at most one plan at a time and 10 an hour.
  Switching meal settings back and forth and reloading the page, which made a plan each time with no limit, now counts
  against the same hour.
  The hourly count alone didn't do this: its lock is released before the planner runs, so a burst of parallel
  requests all passed it and could hold every worker. Refused requests never reach the planner, not even to draw the
  page, and both counters are shared across pods because they live in the database (ADR-006).
* **How the in-flight limit works:** a slot is taken and given back with single `UPDATE` statements on a
  `ThrottleCounter` row (`count < limit` -> `count + 1`; `count - 1` in a `finally`). PostgreSQL rechecks the `WHERE`
  clause against a row another request has just changed, so two requests can't both take the last slot, and no lock or
  transaction stays open while the planner runs. A worker killed mid-run never gives its slot back, so a slot frees
  itself `lease_seconds` (120) after the latest one was taken; that is safely longer than Gunicorn's `--timeout` (60),
  after which any such worker has been killed, and a test fails if the two drift apart.
* **F-2:** a class can sign up and log in together from one campus IP. The assessment's demonstration (20 failed logins
  from one IP, then a refused correct password) now lets the correct password through. Password guessing against one
  account is still capped at 5 attempts per IP per 15 minutes, and spraying from one IP is still bounded. Probing
  which usernames and emails have accounts keeps its old budget of 10 answers per IP per hour: only sign-ups that
  reveal nothing (successes, or errors such as a weak password) get the larger limit.
* **F-4:** after the first HTTPS visit, browsers refuse plain HTTP to `fitpro-app.tech` for the max-age, closing the
  SSL-stripping window on later visits. `manage.py check --deploy` in CI now fails if HSTS is removed, because W004 is no
  longer silenced.
* Each change has a regression test that fails without it (`nutrition.test_views.NewPlanThrottleTests`,
  `NewPlanConcurrencyTests`, and `NewPlanParallelTests` (12 parallel new-plan requests, then 12 parallel first page
  views, from 3 users, on PostgreSQL),
  `accounts.tests.MealPlanSlotTests`, the `accounts.tests` shared-IP tests, `core.tests.StrictTransportSecurityTests`).

## Tradeoffs considered
* **Gunicorn `gthread` workers or more workers (assessment F-1 remediation 3):** rejected for our scope. The planner
  is CPU-bound and holds Python's GIL, so threads add no planning capacity on a pod that requests 250m CPU. Each
  thread also keeps its own database connection (`CONN_MAX_AGE=60`), and the default `db-f1-micro` Cloud SQL tier
  (`terraform_scripts/variables.tf`) allows few connections. The rate limit removes the one-account case the finding describes. Revisit if logs show slow requests
  or failed health probes.
* **Hold a database row lock for the whole planner run (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE NOWAIT`) or a PostgreSQL advisory
  lock instead of counted slots:** rejected. Both free themselves if the worker dies, but they keep a transaction or a
  session-level lock open on a pooled connection (`CONN_MAX_AGE=60`) for the whole CPU-bound run, and SQLite (local
  development) ignores both, so the limit would only exist in production. Counted slots behave the same on both
  databases and need no new table; the cost is the lease, explained above.
* **Only one run per user, no total cap:** rejected. Four accounts could still hold all four workers.
* **Leave page views unlimited ("viewing the plan is not limited", as first proposed):** rejected. Making a plan on a
  page view runs the same planner, and switching settings made it repeatable without limit. Only showing a saved plan
  stays unlimited.
* **Let a refused request wait for a slot:** rejected. A waiting request still holds a sync worker, which is the
  problem being fixed.
* **Caching plans per user, week and inputs (F-1 remediation 2):** rejected. "New meal plan" is meant to return a
  different plan each time, so a cache would break the feature.
* **A time or iteration budget inside the planner (F-1 remediation 2):** not done. The rate limit bounds the total
  cost per user. Worth adding if the recipe catalog grows enough to make single runs slow.
* **Let a correct password through when only the IP counter has tripped (F-2 remediation 1):** rejected. The IP
  counter is the only limit on spraying one password across many accounts, so bypassing it would leave spraying
  bounded only by the per-identifier limit. A CAPTCHA or proof-of-work fallback would need a third-party service; still
  deferred, as in ADR-006.
* **Count only successful registrations (F-2 remediation 3):** rejected for now. Until email verification exists, the
  registration form still says when a username or email is taken (F-3), and the per-IP counters are the only limit on
  that. Every POST is still counted against `register`; the answers that reveal an account are counted again, against
  the lower `register_taken`.
* **Raise `register` alone (as first proposed):** rejected in review. It gave probing for accounts 5 times the old
  budget.
* **Hide which field is taken ("That username or email can't be used"):** not done. It still reveals that one of them
  has an account, and users need to know which to change. An enumeration-safe sign-up needs email verification (F-3).
* **Count a revealing answer after it is sent:** rejected. Parallel probes could all be answered before any was
  counted. It is counted after validation but before the answer goes out, under `acquire`'s row lock, so at most 10
  are answered.
* **Reserve a probe on every POST and give it back when the answer reveals nothing (the first version of this fix):**
  rejected in review. Race-free, but once 10 answers had said "taken", every sign-up from that IP was refused for the
  rest of the hour, including valid new accounts.
* **A plain 429 once the probe budget is used up:** rejected. Sending a deliberately bad password would turn it into a
  yes/no answer: 429 for a taken email, 200 with only the password error for a free one, bringing probing back to the
  50-per-hour budget. `RegisterThrottleTests` checks that the two pages match.
* **HSTS for a year straight away, or with `includeSubDomains` / `preload`:** rejected. Browsers cache HSTS, and
  preload lists are slow to undo, so we start at one hour for this host only.
* **Known limits:**
  * The total cap means a few accounts sending new-plan requests nonstop can make "Generate a new meal plan" answer
    "busy" for everyone else. The rest of the site keeps 2 free workers, which is the point of the finding.
  * The hourly count is shared by both routes, so after 10 plans in an hour a user who changes their meal settings
    sees "no saved plan for your current meal settings" until the hour frees up. Their old plan isn't shown in its
    place, because the "How this plan was made" notes would describe the new settings, not the ones it was made from.
  * The first view of a week with no saved plan is refused with "already being made" if two other users' plans are
    being made at that moment; reloading after `Retry-After` shows it.
  * With SQLite (local development only), the in-flight limit works but isn't exercised in parallel by the tests.
  * Someone sharing an IP with other users can still block logins from it after 100 failures in 15 minutes, or
    sign-ups after 50 POSTs in an hour. After 10 "already taken" answers from one IP in an hour, someone there who
    picks a taken username or email isn't told why: they see their other errors, or the generic "Too many attempts",
    until the hour is up. New accounts with free usernames and emails still sign up.
  * Once the probe budget is used up, a probe with an otherwise valid form still learns something: a free email
    creates an account (and signs the prober in) and a taken one doesn't. Each such probe costs a real account and
    one of the 50 registration attempts. Closing it needs email verification (F-3, F-5).
  * F-3 (registration reveals taken usernames and emails) and F-5 (email ownership is never verified) need a
    production email backend and are tracked separately.
  * The rest of F-4 is out of this change: a Content-Security-Policy, `Cache-Control: no-store` on signed-in pages, and
    the `server: gunicorn` banner. The README known gaps list them.
